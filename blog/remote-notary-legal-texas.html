Are Remote Notaries Legal in Texas?

2026-10-09

Yes, remote online notarization (RON) is legal in Texas. State law allows certain documents to be notarized through a live video call with a commissioned online notary, rather than meeting in person. That said, not every document or situation is a good fit for a remote session, which is why many people still choose an in-person mobile notary instead.

What remote online notarization actually means

With RON, you connect with a notary through a secure video platform. You show your ID on camera, sign the document electronically, and the notary applies an electronic seal and signature. It's convenient for routine paperwork when both parties are comfortable with technology and the document type is eligible for remote signing.

How it's different from a mobile notary visit

A mobile notary, on the other hand, travels to you — your home, office, hospital room, or another location in person. This matters for documents that require witnesses, physical ink signatures, or extra care with an older or less tech-comfortable signer. It also helps when someone simply prefers a face-to-face appointment over a video call.

We've written more about this distinction in our post comparing a notary vs. a mobile notary, if you want the fuller breakdown.

When an in-person mobile notary makes more sense

  • You're organizing estate, healthcare, or power-of-attorney documents as part of a broader planning effort
  • The signer is more comfortable with paper and a live person in the room
  • Internet access or video call quality isn't reliable at the location
  • The document calls for a witness or extra verification steps

If any of these apply, having someone come to your house or office in Plano, Frisco, Allen, or Richardson is often the simpler path.

Things to check before choosing either option

Before you book a remote or mobile notary, confirm that the specific document type is eligible for the notarization method you're considering, and make sure your ID is current and matches the name on the document. If you're unsure which route fits your situation, it's worth asking before your appointment rather than during it.
